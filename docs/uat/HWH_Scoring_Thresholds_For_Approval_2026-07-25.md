## Why you are being asked this now

During testing you filled in the assessment with mild answers and it came back
recommending Integration, the top tier. You were right that this is wrong.

The reason is not a coding slip. Back on 5 July a rule was proposed for how to
turn assessment answers into a recommended tier: look at how *many* areas of the
body are affected, look at how *severe* the answers are, and take **whichever of
the two points higher**. That proposal was written down with a note saying it was
waiting for your answer. Your answer was never given, and the rule was built
anyway.

So what you saw in testing is that unanswered proposal, working exactly as it was
written. Someone with mild symptoms spread across many areas hits the "many
areas" half of the rule and gets sent to the top tier, regardless of how mild
their answers were.

We are not going to repeat that. Below is a replacement rule with real numbers.
Nothing gets built until you say yes, or change the numbers.

## What the assessment already collects

Two things matter for this decision:

**How you rate each area, 1 to 10.** There are 29 of these ratings in the
assessment. **10 means no trouble at all, 1 means severe trouble.** Higher is
better. This is worth pausing on, because it is the opposite of how most people
read a severity score.

**How many focus areas come out of it.** After the assessment, a roadmap is built
out of specific focus areas, for example Gut Lining Permeability or Blood Sugar
Stability. Someone might get 2 focus areas, someone else 9.

---

## The proposed rule

**Severity decides the tier. Breadth can raise it by one step, but only when the
severity is already moderate or worse.**

That single sentence is the change. Today breadth can send a mild person to the
top tier on its own. Under this rule it cannot.

### Step 1, severity sets the starting tier

Based on the average of their 1 to 10 ratings:

| Average rating | What it means | Starting tier |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 8.0 and above | Very little trouble anywhere | Awareness, $9 |
| 6.5 to 7.9 | Mild, noticeable but manageable | Foundation, $29 |
| 5.0 to 6.4 | Moderate, affecting daily life | Guided, $69 |
| 3.5 to 4.9 | Significant, affecting most days | Restoration, $119 |
| Below 3.5 | Severe across the board | Integration, $299 |

### Step 2, breadth can raise it one step

| Number of focus areas | Effect |
| --- | --- |
| 1 to 5 | No change |
| 6 or more | Move up one tier, **but only if the starting tier was Guided or higher** |

So a person whose ratings average 4.2 across 7 focus areas starts at Restoration
and moves up to Integration. A person whose ratings average 7.5 across 9 focus
areas stays at Foundation, because mild is mild no matter how widely it is
spread.

### What this does to the case you found

Your test answers were mild across many areas. Today that returns **Integration**.
Under this rule averages of 8.0 and above return **Awareness**, and averages in
the 6.5 to 7.9 band return **Foundation**, regardless of how many focus areas
appear. If that still feels too high or too low to you, the numbers in the
tables are the dials to turn.

## The one judgement call we want you to look at hardest

The rule above says breadth never lifts a mild person. There is an argument
against that: someone with mild symptoms in nine different systems may genuinely
need more help than someone with mild symptoms in one, because the pattern is
widespread even if no single symptom is loud.

We have proposed the stricter version, where mild stays mild, for two reasons.
It matches what you said in testing. And recommending a $299 tier to someone
reporting mild symptoms damages trust in the recommendation itself, which is the
part of the product doing the selling.

If you disagree, the alternative is: breadth of 8 or more lifts any tier by one
step, so mild plus very broad becomes Guided at $69 rather than Foundation at $29.
Say the word and we will build that instead.

## The second thing on the same screen: pattern labels

Separately from the tier, the results page labels each pattern it finds as high,
medium or low. You saw mild answers producing medium and high labels.

Proposed, using the same 1 to 10 ratings, applied to each individual area rather
than the average:

| Their rating for that area | Label shown |
| --- | --- |
| 8 to 10 | Low |
| 5 to 7 | Medium |
| 1 to 4 | High |

This is being fixed in the same batch as the tier rule, deliberately. If only the
tier were fixed, you would run a mild assessment, see a sensible tier sitting next
to pattern cards still marked high, and correctly mark the whole thing failed.
The two are judged together or not at all.
